Rules 101

Pickleball Scoring Explained

How side-out scoring actually works, how to call the score in doubles and singles without hesitating, and why three major pro tours now play under three different scoring systems.

Pickleball scoring trips people up for one simple reason: for most of the sport's history, only the serving side could actually score a point. That's the opposite of how tennis, volleyball, or basically any rally sport most people grew up with works. Once that clicks, the rest — calling the score, tracking whose serve it is, keeping up with doubles' extra wrinkle — falls into place fast. And as of 2026, there's a second layer worth knowing: rally scoring, where every rally scores a point, is now legal and in real use at the pro level, so you'll increasingly see both systems depending on where you're watching or playing.

Side-Out Scoring: The Core Idea

Traditional pickleball scoring is called side-out scoring, and it's still the default for recreational play and for USA Pickleball's biggest sanctioned events. The rule that matters most: only the serving side can score a point. If the serving side wins the rally, they score a point and keep serving. If the receiving side wins the rally, no one scores — instead, the serve passes to the other side (a "side-out"), and the rally restarts at 0-0 on the scoreboard for who scores next.

Games are played to 11 points, and you have to win by at least 2. If the score reaches 10-10, play continues past 11 until one side is ahead by 2 (12-10, 13-11, and so on).

How to Call the Score in Doubles

Before every serve, the server calls the score out loud as three numbers: the serving team's score, the receiving team's score, and which server on the team is currently serving — 1 or 2. A score call of "6-3-2" means the serving team has 6 points, the receiving team has 3, and it's the second server on the serving team who's about to serve.

That third number exists because of doubles' extra wrinkle: each team normally gets two server turns per side-out, one for each partner, before the serve passes to the other team. When the first server on a team loses a rally, the serve doesn't go to the other team yet — it passes to that team's second server, who keeps serving until their side also loses a rally. Only then does the serve cross over to the opposing team.

There's one deliberate exception, and it's why you'll hear a match start with "0-0-2" instead of "0-0-1": at the very start of a game, the very first serving team only gets one server turn before losing the serve, not the usual two. It's a built-in handicap so the team that serves first doesn't get an extra, unearned possession.

Singles Scoring: Simpler, With One Twist

Singles uses the same side-out logic — only the server scores — but drops the third number entirely, since there's no partner to track. A singles score is just called as two numbers: server's score, then receiver's score (e.g., "5-3"), and the server keeps serving until they lose a rally, at which point it's a side-out and the serve passes directly to the opponent.

The one twist beginners miss: in singles, which side of the court you serve from depends on your own score, not a fixed rotation. If your score is even (including 0), you serve from the right/even side of the court; if your score is odd, you serve from the left/odd side. Get in the habit of glancing at the scoreboard before you walk to a side, and it becomes automatic within a few games.

Rally Scoring: The Other System You'll See in 2026

Rally scoring flips the core rule: every rally scores a point for someone, regardless of who served. Win the rally as the receiver and you get both the point and the serve. Because points come faster, rally-scored games are usually played to a higher target — 15 or 21 points, still win-by-2 — rather than the 11 points used in side-out.

For most of pickleball's growth, rally scoring was essentially an unofficial or exhibition-only format. That changed in 2025, when USA Pickleball's rulebook legalized rally scoring as an optional, provisional format — and as of the 2026 rulebook, it remains provisional while USA Pickleball collects more data before deciding whether to keep, adjust, or drop it. Tournament directors can currently offer rally-scored formats (one game, or best-of-3 or best-of-5, to 11, 15, or 21 points) for most events, with specific exceptions: rally scoring isn't permitted at 2026 Golden Ticket events, the 2026 USA Pickleball National Championships, or double-elimination doubles brackets, which all stay on traditional side-out scoring.

Why the Pro Tours Don't All Play the Same Way

If you've watched pickleball on TV or streaming and the scoring felt different match to match, you weren't imagining it — the three biggest professional circuits currently use three different approaches:

  • PPA Tour plays under the 2026 USA Pickleball framework using rally scoring for much of its event slate.
  • APP Tour has stuck with traditional side-out scoring.
  • Major League Pickleball (MLP) actually reversed course: it launched with full rally scoring back in 2023, then moved its doubles formats (gender and mixed doubles) back to traditional side-out scoring, to 11 points win-by-2, for the 2026 season. Rally scoring didn't disappear from MLP entirely, though — it now lives in the "DreamBreaker" tiebreaker, a fast-paced format played to 21 points with players rotating out every four points.

None of this affects how you should keep score at your local courts — side-out scoring to 11 is still the near-universal default for casual and league play. But if a friend mentions rally scoring or you notice a pro match playing to 15 or 21, now you know exactly what's changed and why.

Quick Reference

  • Side-out scoring: only the server scores. Games to 11, win by 2.
  • Doubles score call: serving score - receiving score - server number (1 or 2). First serving team of the game only gets one server ("0-0-2" start).
  • Singles score call: server's score - receiver's score. Serve from the right side on an even score, left side on odd.
  • Rally scoring: every rally scores a point for someone. Usually played to 15 or 21, still win by 2. Provisional under USA Pickleball's rulebook through 2026, and not used at Golden Ticket events, Nationals, or double-elimination doubles.

Key Takeaways

  • Side-out scoring's core rule: only the serving side can score a point. Games go to 11, win by 2.
  • Doubles score calls use three numbers because each team gets two server turns per side-out — except the very first serve of the game, which is a one-server exception ("0-0-2").
  • Singles drops the third number and adds a twist: serve from the right on an even score, left on an odd score.
  • Rally scoring (a point every rally, games to 15 or 21) is legal but still provisional under USA Pickleball's 2026 rulebook, and barred from Golden Ticket events, Nationals, and double-elimination doubles.
  • PPA Tour uses rally scoring, APP Tour uses side-out, and MLP reverted its doubles to side-out for 2026 while keeping rally scoring only in its DreamBreaker tiebreaker.
